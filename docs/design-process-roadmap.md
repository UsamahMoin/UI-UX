## Research first, not another portfolio tile

The strongest next increment is to deepen existing work:

1. **LUMEN:** observe a consenting team's planning ritual, run the paper worktable, record what changes, then revise the digital prototype. Deliver observation notes, an affinity synthesis, annotated low-fidelity iterations, and a task-based evaluation.
2. **CIVIC:** work with a willing community organization on one real request type. Map the resident experience and staff handoff together. Deliver a service blueprint and evidence-backed journey, with consent and anonymized excerpts. No real request sending or organizational outreach is authorized by this roadmap.
3. **FORM:** run a small taxonomy study and separate browsing from retrieval tasks. Deliver the grouping evidence, a revised information architecture, and a comparison of task outcomes before and after the change.

An additional project becomes useful only if access to a real setting exposes a distinct need. One candidate is a community equipment-lending service: discover how borrowing, pickup, return, maintenance, and staff ownership work before building a screen. It could demonstrate physical/digital handoffs that the present prototypes do not validate. This is an opportunity brief, not a new commissioned project or a claim that research has begun.

## Choose methods by the question

Interviews can explore a recent concrete decision; contextual inquiry can reveal workarounds during an actual task; diary or cultural-probe activities can reveal patterns over time. A focus group is an option for discussing shared norms, but it should not substitute for observing individual usability. Surveys require a clear question and a defensible recruitment approach before percentages become meaningful. Social listening and keyword research could inform FORM or SCRIBBLE language, provided the source, context, privacy limits, and sampling limitations are recorded. None of these activities has been claimed as completed here.

## Evidence record for future studies

For each study, retain: the question, method and rationale, recruitment criteria, consent and privacy arrangements, protocol, raw observations, synthesis, design implications, alternatives considered, prototype changes, evaluation tasks, outcomes, and limitations. Separate what participants did from the designer's interpretation. Preserve unsuccessful iterations as well as the final design. Never retrofit a fabricated discovery phase around finished visuals.
